## Purpose
Collect comparable, decision-relevant information about the organization, offer, audience, market, current brand, and constraints before synthesis. The questionnaire supplements interviews; it does not establish market truth by itself.

## Inputs
Organization and offer description; intended respondent(s); decision this discovery will support; accessible existing research; privacy/retention policy; requested completion time.

## Procedure
1. Define the decision and respondent groups. Ask only questions that could affect a brand or business decision.
2. Draft sections: organization and goals; products/services and proof; current and desired audiences; customer problems and alternatives; perceived differentiation; voice and identity; constraints and risk.
3. Use neutral wording. Prefer one idea per question; distinguish factual fields from opinions. Include “not sure,” “not applicable,” and optional free-text where useful. Avoid forced rankings without a clear comparison set.
4. For every claimed customer or market fact, ask for source, date, sample or basis, and confidence. Label unsupported statements as hypotheses.
5. Pilot with one or two representative respondents. Check comprehension, completion time, skipped items, and whether answer options omit common cases. Revise before broad use.
6. Distribute with purpose, expected time, audience, voluntary participation, data use, access, retention, and contact for questions. Collect the minimum personal data required.
7. Review responses for missingness, duplicates, contradictory answers, and respondent-role effects. Do not average unlike populations without explanation.
8. Summarize patterns and dissent separately. Preserve source links and representative anonymized evidence where consent permits.

## Output
Versioned questionnaire; respondent and sampling note; response dataset or qualitative notes; completion/missingness summary; findings separated into observed data, interpretation, and hypothesis; limitations and follow-up questions.

## Acceptance and failure
Pass if each question maps to the stated decision, pilot findings are resolved, privacy/consent language is present, and the summary reports sample and limitations. Fail if the instrument is leading, collects unnecessary sensitive data, claims representativeness without a sampling basis, or treats self-report as verified behavior. Stop distribution until those issues are corrected.
