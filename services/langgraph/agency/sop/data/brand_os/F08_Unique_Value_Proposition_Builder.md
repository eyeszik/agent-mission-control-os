## Purpose
Translate a validated positioning territory into a clear, specific, supportable value proposition that helps the intended audience understand why the offer is relevant and credible. A UVP is a strategic statement to test, not a guarantee of uniqueness or performance.

## Inputs
Selected F07 territory; defined audience and use case; offer features and outcomes; proof and limitations; alternatives; required channels; legal/regulatory constraints; F06 confidence ledger.

## Procedure
1. Restate the audience, situation, problem or desired progress, and alternative they use today. Resolve ambiguity before writing.
2. List outcomes the offer can actually deliver. Separate features, benefits, emotional outcomes, and claims requiring evidence.
3. Identify the strongest differentiator supported by F04/F05 and organization capabilities. If uniqueness is unverified, describe it as a candidate distinction, not an objective fact.
4. Draft multiple propositions using plain language. A working structure is: “For [audience] facing [need], [offer] helps [outcome] through [credible difference/proof].” Treat this as a scaffold, not a mandatory formula.
5. Attach proof to each material claim: product evidence, customer evidence, measured results, process, or qualified expertise. Remove or qualify claims without evidence.
6. Check specificity, relevance, comprehension, differentiation, credibility, and consistency across channels. Avoid vague superlatives, guarantees, and unsupported comparative claims.
7. Test with representative audience members using comprehension and preference questions. Measure whether they correctly identify audience, benefit, and reason to believe; record sample and method.
8. Revise once evidence is considered; preserve variants, test conditions, and unresolved claims. Route regulated, comparative, or high-risk claims for qualified review before publication.

## Output
Three to five candidate UVPs; recommended version or no-decision; audience and channel; evidence/proof map; claim caveats; test plan and results; confidence; review needs.

## Acceptance and stop conditions
Pass when target audience and outcome are explicit, differentiation is supportable, claims have proof or qualification, and the proposition passes a comprehension check. Stop publication if claims are unsubstantiated, legal review is pending where required, or customer testing contradicts the proposition.
