## Purpose
Develop and test a strategic position the organization could credibly seek to own in a category. “Ownership” means a distinctive, repeatable association in the target audience’s mind; it is an aspiration until customer evidence validates it.

## Inputs
F01 founder principles and constraints; F03 customer desires; F04 competitive audit; F05 category opportunities; F06 confidence ledger; business capabilities, proof, and strategic goals.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the audience, category frame, desired customer progress, and strategic horizon. Identify unresolved critical evidence from F06.
2. Generate several position territories from a specific audience need, a meaningful category tension, and an organizational capability or belief. Do not begin with a slogan or visual style.
3. For each territory, state: audience; need; category frame; distinctive point of view; promised progress; proof available; competitive overlap; operational implications; and risks.
4. Test credibility: can the organization deliver the promise repeatedly with current or realistically buildable capabilities? Identify proof gaps and required investments.
5. Test differentiation against F04 and relevance against F03. Use customer feedback or structured research for claims about audience perception.
6. Check coherence with F01 and category attractiveness in F05. Surface tensions instead of smoothing them over.
7. Score territories with disclosed criteria: relevance, distinctiveness, credibility, evidence, strategic fit, durability, and feasibility. Keep critical dimensions separate; do not let an average hide a fatal weakness.
8. Select a recommended territory only if it clears minimum evidence and feasibility thresholds. Otherwise present alternatives and tests.

## Output
Positioning territory set; evidence-linked comparison; recommended territory or explicit no-decision; proof plan; strategic trade-offs; audience validation plan; confidence and open risks.

## Acceptance
Pass when the recommended territory is relevant, differentiable, credible, and traceable to F01–F06 evidence, with a defined validation method. Stop if differentiation is only aesthetic, promise lacks delivery proof, or the audience/category is unresolved. Do not claim “category ownership” before behavioral or perceptual evidence supports it.
