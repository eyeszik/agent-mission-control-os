## Purpose
Map customer goals, situations, pains, desired progress, emotional and social outcomes, alternatives, barriers, and evidence. The map is a research synthesis, not a substitute for customer research or a claim that all customers are alike.

## Inputs
Defined audience segment and inclusion criteria; customer interviews, support records, reviews, surveys, analytics or field observations with provenance; time period; consent/privacy limits; decision to inform.

## Procedure
1. Define the segment, context, and research question. Do not merge materially different users to make a single persona.
2. Inventory evidence with source, date, method, sample, and limitations. Separate direct customer statements and observed behavior from researcher interpretation.
3. Code evidence into situation/trigger, functional goal, emotional goal, social goal, desired progress, pain, workaround/alternative, barrier, and success signal. Keep quotations exact and de-identified where appropriate.
4. Build a desire map for each coherent segment. Attach each claim to evidence IDs and note counterexamples.
5. Rate evidence strength using the F06 ledger; mark thinly supported themes as hypotheses. Avoid invented percentages or false precision.
6. Identify unmet or poorly served needs only where evidence shows importance and current alternatives fail or impose meaningful cost.
7. Validate themes with a suitable follow-up sample or stakeholder review. Ask what would disconfirm each major interpretation.
8. Translate findings into opportunity statements without prescribing a solution prematurely.

## Output
Segment definition; evidence register; desire map; ranked needs with rationale; alternative/workaround map; barriers; counterevidence; confidence; opportunity hypotheses; validation questions.

## Quality gate
Pass when each material desire has traceable evidence, segment boundaries are explicit, contradictions are retained, and findings distinguish observation from interpretation. Stop if source provenance or consent is unclear, or the evidence cannot support customer-level generalization. Never fabricate customer quotes or imply statistical representativeness from convenience samples.
