## Purpose
Maintain an auditable register of material claims, sources, methods, uncertainty, counterevidence, and confidence so downstream brand decisions do not turn assumptions into facts.

## Inputs
Claims from F01–F05 and other research; source artifacts; source dates and methods; decision context; confidence scale; reviewer.

## Procedure
1. Atomize claims. One row should express one testable statement and its scope (who/what/where/when).
2. Record claim ID, exact wording, claim type (observation, self-report, interpretation, estimate, forecast), source locator, source owner, date, method, sample/coverage, and access status.
3. Assess source quality across directness, independence, applicability, recency, completeness, and potential bias. Explain the rating; do not rely on source prestige alone.
4. Record supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, missing evidence, and plausible alternative explanations.
5. Assign a qualitative confidence level: HIGH (multiple relevant, independent and consistent sources or direct robust measurement); MEDIUM (credible but limited evidence or some unresolved counterevidence); LOW (single, indirect, stale, or materially incomplete evidence); UNVERIFIED (no adequate evidence). Confidence is not a probability unless calibrated data supports one.
6. Define a verification action, owner, deadline or event trigger, and decision impact if false. Set a review date for volatile claims.
7. Update the ledger when evidence changes; preserve prior status and reason for change.
8. Before a decision, filter for claims that are critical, low-confidence, stale, contradicted, or dependent on inaccessible sources. Escalate those claims or narrow the decision.

## Output
Versioned claim ledger; source log; confidence rationale; counterevidence; unresolved questions; verification queue; change history.

## Acceptance and stop
Pass when every material recommendation traces to claim IDs and sources, uncertainties are visible, and no confidence rating is unsupported. Stop a high-impact recommendation if a critical claim is unverified or a decisive source is inaccessible. Never convert “not found” into “false” or “does not exist.”
