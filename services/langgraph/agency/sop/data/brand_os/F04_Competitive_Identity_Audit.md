## Purpose
Compare how a defined set of competitors present their identities and offers, and identify credible areas of similarity, difference, and possible whitespace. This is a bounded desk-research audit, not a legal clearance or complete market census.

## Inputs
Category and geography; customer segment; competitor inclusion rule; direct, indirect, and substitute competitors; audit date; source-access policy; decision to support.

## Procedure
1. Define the comparison frame and select competitors using a stated rule (for example, customer alternatives or category search visibility). Include substitutes where relevant.
2. Gather current, public primary materials: official websites, product pages, pricing, packaging, public campaigns, and public company statements. Record URL, capture date, market, and access limitations.
3. Record observable identity dimensions: stated promise, audience, category language, proof, offer, price cues, tone, visual codes, channels, and calls to action. Distinguish quoted claims from analyst interpretation.
4. Cross-check material claims across more than one primary artifact where possible. Label inaccessible, stale, or promotional claims as such.
5. Compare patterns in a matrix. Mark common conventions, distinct positions, and apparent gaps; do not infer customer preference from competitor messaging alone.
6. Identify potential conflicts, trademark/name risks, regulated claims, and copied expression for qualified legal review; do not make a legal conclusion.
7. Test proposed whitespace against customer evidence (F03), category evidence (F05), and confidence (F06). Record disconfirming examples.

## Output
Scope and competitor list; dated source log; comparison matrix; pattern analysis; candidate differentiation territories; unsupported assumptions; risks and follow-up research.

## Acceptance and stop conditions
Pass when competitors were selected by a disclosed rule, every row has dated sources, observations and interpretations are separate, and claims are qualified. Stop if the category or geography is undefined, sources cannot be verified, or a proposed “open space” depends only on sparse web coverage. Do not reproduce competitor assets beyond short, attributed analytical references.
