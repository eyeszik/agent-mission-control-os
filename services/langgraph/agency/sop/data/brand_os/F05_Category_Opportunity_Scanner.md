## Purpose
Assess whether a category or adjacent market contains a plausible, evidence-supported opportunity for the organization. The scan produces testable hypotheses, not a market-size guarantee or investment recommendation.

## Inputs
Product/service concept; target geography and time horizon; audience and use case; known category definitions; available primary data; organization capabilities and constraints; required decision.

## Procedure
1. Define category boundaries, adjacent categories, substitutes, geography, and customer/job context. State what is excluded.
2. Gather dated evidence from authoritative primary sources where possible: public statistics, regulatory data, company filings, pricing/catalogs, customer research, and direct interviews. Note methodology and coverage.
3. Examine demand signals: frequency and severity of the problem, existing spend or workarounds, search or usage evidence where valid, adoption barriers, and customer willingness-to-switch evidence.
4. Examine supply and structure: incumbents, substitutes, concentration where measurable, channel access, switching costs, regulations, and unmet segments.
5. Build opportunity hypotheses as: target segment + unmet progress + why current options underperform + evidence + proposed test.
6. Estimate market ranges only with explicit definitions and reproducible inputs. Separate TAM/SAM/SOM if requested; show assumptions and sensitivity instead of a single unsupported figure.
7. Score opportunities on customer importance, evidence strength, differentiation potential, feasibility, economics, and risk. Publish weights and confidence; do not hide low-scoring dimensions in an aggregate.
8. Define the cheapest credible validation experiment and a disconfirmation threshold for each leading hypothesis.

## Output
Category scope; evidence register; demand/supply analysis; opportunity hypotheses; transparent scoring; rough sizing with assumptions (if data permits); risks; validation experiments; no-go or insufficient-evidence findings.

## Acceptance
Pass when every material claim is linked to dated evidence, category boundaries are explicit, estimates can be recalculated, and at least one disconfirmation test exists. Stop if source quality cannot support sizing or the decision depends on unverified access, legal, or economic assumptions. A “scan” does not establish commercial viability.
